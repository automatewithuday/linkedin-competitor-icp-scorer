# Battle Card: The Assumed-Knowledge Prompt

Use this when you have a ranked engager who left a comment - especially one that shows topic fluency. The goal is to open with a reference that proves you were in the same room, not a cold pitch that forces them to explain their context to you.

---

## The principle

Most cold outreach opens with "I help companies like yours..." - which immediately flags you as someone who doesn't know them. Assumed-knowledge outreach opens mid-conversation. You reference the thing they engaged with. You name their situation without asking about it. They experience you as a peer, not a vendor.

This only works when you actually know the context. The ranked CSV gives you the context.

---

## The prompt (use this to write your first message)

```
You are writing a cold LinkedIn message (connection request note OR first DM).

Context:
- Prospect: [name], [title] at [company]
- They engaged with: [post topic / content theme from your harvest run]
- Their comment (if any): "[comment_excerpt from CSV]"
- What we sell: [your one-line description]
- Their likely pain: [the problem your ICP has that maps to this content]

Rules:
- Open by referencing the post or topic they engaged with. Don't say "I saw you liked X" - reference the idea, not the action.
- Name their pain or situation in the second sentence. Don't ask if they have it.
- One sentence on what you do - category, not product name.
- Close with the smallest possible next step. Not "let's hop on a call." Something like "worth a quick note back?"
- Max 5 sentences total.
- No em-dashes, no exclamation marks, no "I hope this finds you well."

Write the message.
```

---

## Example output (generic)

> "Caught your comment on [founder]'s post about [topic] - that tension between [pain A] and [pain B] is exactly what makes this hard to solve at scale. Most teams in your position end up doing [workaround] which buys time but doesn't fix the underlying issue. We built [product] specifically for that problem. Worth a quick note back if it's still live for you?"

---

## When to use it

- `intent = high` rows in your CSV - they named a problem in their comment
- `icp_fit >= 4` - title and industry match
- Comment excerpt contains a question, a frustration, or a direct mention of the category you sell into

Skip it when the comment is generic praise ("Great post!") - use the Pain-Pivot template instead.
