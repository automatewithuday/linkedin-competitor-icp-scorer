# Positioning Play: Line in the Sand

The engager already has a vendor, a tool, or a current approach. Your job isn't to attack that choice - it's to name the category shift that makes their current approach obsolete, and position yourself on the right side of the line.

---

## The principle

Buyers who engage with competitor content aren't in a vacuum. They're using something. They liked the post because the topic resonated - which means the pain is real. The positioning play is to make the category decision obvious without naming the competitor.

Name what changed. Draw the line. Stand on one side of it.

---

## The framework

**Step 1 - Name the shift**
Something changed in the world (technology, market structure, buyer behavior, regulation) that makes the old approach incomplete. This is the "before/after." You're not saying the old way is wrong - you're saying the world moved.

Example: "When outbound lists were built manually, volume was the only lever. Now that engagement is trackable at the individual level, volume is the wrong lever."

**Step 2 - Name the category you're creating or owning**
Not your product. The problem-framing that favors your product. The prospect should be able to agree with the category before they agree to talk to you.

Example: "Engagement-based prospecting" vs. "cold list prospecting."

**Step 3 - The small ask**
One question that lets them self-identify. Not "are you interested?" but something that assumes they're already thinking about this.

Example: "Are you tracking which competitors' audiences are actually in-market?"

---

## Application to this tool

The shift: LinkedIn engagement has always been visible, but extracting and scoring it at scale wasn't possible for most teams. Now it is. The line is between teams who prospect from static lists vs. teams who prospect from live behavioral signals.

The message: "The list you built last quarter doesn't know who raised their hand last week. This does."

---

## When to use this framing

- When the prospect's title suggests they're already doing outbound (VP Sales, Head of Growth, Founder)
- When you're moving someone away from a competing approach, not just adding to their stack
- When `intent = medium` - they're engaged but not screaming a specific pain; you need to sharpen the frame for them

Don't use it as a cold opener. Use it in the second message after you've gotten a reply, or as the positioning layer in a longer sequence.
